Geospatial Context & Big-Data Architecture

The QuakeSpots GIS Portal is a specialized geospatial intelligence application engineered to evaluate 68,545 recorded seismic events spanning the Philippine archipelago (4°N–22°N latitude, 116°E–128°E longitude).

The Philippine territory occupies an exceptionally active convergent tectonic margin bounded by the Philippine Trench to the east and the Manila Trench, Negros Trench, and Cotabato Trench to the west, bisected by the 1,200 km left-lateral strike-slip Philippine Fault System (PFZ). This portal applies rigorous spatial econometric and point-pattern algorithms to transform raw seismic telemetry into actionable disaster risk intelligence.

  • Big-Data Ingestion: 68,545 seismic event records containing spatial coordinates, hypocenter depth, moment magnitude, and timestamps.
  • Zero-Latency Web Cartography: Layer-controlled Leaflet runtime with Esri Dark Gray Canvas basemaps and dynamic clustering.
  • Multi-scale Spatial Statistics: Integration of local spatial autocorrelation, space-time binning, and kernel smoothing.

Getis-Ord Gi* Spatial Statistics (Hot Spot Analysis)

Identifies statistically significant spatial clusters of high magnitude seismic events (hot spots) and low magnitude tremors (cold spots) using the local Getis-Ord Gi* statistic:

functions Spatial Hot Spot Statistic Standardized Normal Score \( z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \)
\[ G_i^*(d) = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^n w_{ij}(d) x_j - \bar{X} \sum_{j=1}^n w_{ij}(d)}{S \sqrt{\dfrac{n \displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^n w_{ij}^2(d) - \left(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^n w_{ij}(d)\right)^2}{n - 1}}} \]
Numerator · Observed Local Discrepancy Local weighted sum \( \sum w_{ij} x_j \) minus expected random sum \( \bar{X}\sum w_{ij} \). Measures how much the local magnitude neighborhood deviates from global randomness.
Denominator · Spatial Standard Error Global standard deviation \( S \) multiplied by the geometric variance of spatial weights under the null hypothesis of complete spatial randomness (CSR).
Parameter Mathematical & Geospatial Definition Project Value / Role
\( G_i^*(d) \) Local Getis-Ord standardized z-score for seismic event \( i \) at distance cutoff \( d \) Standardized Z-Score
\( x_j \) Moment magnitude (\( M_w \)) of seismic event \( j \) Continuous Variable
\( w_{ij}(d) \) Spatial weight between event \( i \) and \( j \) based on distance threshold \( d \) SciPy KD-Tree Matrix
\( \bar{X} \) Global mean magnitude across all catalog events \( \bar{X} \approx 4.31 \)
\( S \) Global sample standard deviation of magnitudes \( S \approx 0.74 \)
\( n \) Total population of Philippine earthquake records evaluated 68,545 records

Statistical Significance & Confidence Thresholds:

\( z > +2.58 \) · \( p < 0.01 \)
Hot Spot (99% Confidence): Statistically significant cluster of severe, high-magnitude swarms along active trenches and subduction zones (crimson markers).
\( -1.96 \le z \le +1.96 \)
Not Significant: Background seismic distribution consistent with spatial randomness without distinct clustering.
\( z < -2.58 \) · \( p < 0.01 \)
Cold Spot (99% Confidence): Statistically significant cluster of minor low-magnitude micro-tremors (deep blue markers).

Anselin Local Moran's I (LISA) Spatial Autocorrelation

Detects spatial autocorrelation and local spatial outliers by classifying earthquake events into four distinct spatial association quadrants:

High-High (HH) — True Hot Spot High magnitude earthquake clustered in high-magnitude vicinity. Signals major rupture zones with intense stress transfer.
Low-Low (LL) — Cold Cluster Minor tremors surrounded by minor tremors. Represents background micro-seismicity or creeping fault segments.
High-Low (HL) — Spatial Outlier Isolated high-magnitude earthquake surrounded by low values. Indicates isolated stress release or deep slab subduction ruptures.
Low-High (LH) — Quiescent Outlier Low-magnitude event surrounded by high values. May represent a seismic gap accumulating elastic strain adjacent to an active cluster.
hub Local Moran's Statistic Spatial Autocorrelation Index \( I_i \)
\[ I_i = \frac{z_i - \bar{z}}{s^2} \sum_{j=1}^n w_{ij} (z_j - \bar{z}) \]
Parameter Geospatial Definition Role in Algorithm
\( I_i \) Local Moran's autocorrelation index for earthquake epicenter \( i \) Quadrant Classifier
\( z_i - \bar{z} \) Magnitude deviation of focal earthquake \( i \) relative to the nationwide mean Focal Magnitude Offset
\( s^2 \) Sample variance of seismic magnitudes across all Philippine catalog records Variance Normalizer
\( w_{ij} \) Row-standardized spatial contiguity / distance weight matrix element Spatial Neighbor Weight

Emerging Hot Spot Analysis (EHSA) Space-Time Bins

Evaluates temporal persistence and intensification of earthquake clusters by constructing a 3D Space-Time Cube. Clusters are evaluated across historical epochs:

  • Intensifying Hot Spot: A statistically significant hot spot for 90% of the time-step intervals, where magnitude intensity is systematically increasing.
  • Persistent Hot Spot: Continuous spatial clustering of high-magnitude activity over consecutive decades without significant acceleration.
  • Sporadic Hot Spot: Intermittent seismic triggers that fluctuate between significant and non-significant clustering states.
  • Historical Cold Spot: Long-term low-activity bins that denote seismic quiescence along locked fault interfaces.

Dual Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)

Generates continuous probability density surfaces across two separate statistical weightings:

  • Spatial Frequency (Event Density): Pure occurrence rate quantifying where earthquakes happen most frequently regardless of energy.
  • Moment Magnitude Energy Release: Energy-weighted density via seismic moment release \( M_0 \) modeled through the Gutenberg-Richter energy equation:
bolt Gutenberg-Richter Relation Radiated Seismic Energy \( E \) in Joules
\[ \log_{10}(E) = 4.8 + 1.5 M \implies E = 10^{4.8 + 1.5 M} \text{ Joules} \]
Logarithmic Energy Scaling Every \( +1.0 \) increase in moment magnitude scales radiated energy by \( 10^{1.5} \approx 31.62\times \).
Geospatial Hazard Implication A single \( M7.0 \) tremor releases more destructive energy than thirty-one \( M6.0 \) events combined, justifying energy-weighted KDE over simple count density.

Backend Python Scientific & GIS Pipeline

The spatial intelligence pipeline was implemented using Python scientific and geospatial libraries (SciPy, NumPy, GeoPandas, and Folium):

# Fast spatial neighbor lookup using SciPy KD-Tree import numpy as np from scipy.spatial import cKDTree coords = np.radians(df[['latitude', 'longitude']].values) tree = cKDTree(coords) neighbors = tree.query_ball_tree(tree, r=0.015) # Getis-Ord Gi* calculation over 68,545 events gi_z_scores = [] for i, nbrs in enumerate(neighbors): sum_x_j = sum(magnitudes[j] for j in nbrs) sum_w_ij = len(nbrs) numerator = sum_x_j - mean_mag * sum_w_ij denominator = np.sqrt(global_var) * np.sqrt((n * sum_w_ij - sum_w_ij**2) / (n - 1)) gi_z_scores.append(numerator / denominator if denominator != 0 else 0.0)

The spatial outputs were exported into a self-contained Leaflet cartographic visualization with high-performance vector rendering and custom layer toggles.
